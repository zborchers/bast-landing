You're more energy than matter

Physical health always mirrors energetic health.

We're in the midst of an evolutionary moment that's transforming how we think about our body (and our health). For generations we've treated the body as a purely physical object — something to measure, medicate, and manage — but that model of health is expanding into something more comprehensive, and more whole. In order to heal the body, we have to understand, and accept, that the body is literally formed from energy. We are physical beings, yes, but we also exist simultaneously across many planes of existence, all of them rooted in the energetic world, regardless of whether it's emotional, mental, or spiritual. All of the energetic parts of you, along with the physical, are comprehensively interconnected and woven together. They're not separated into parts, nor should they be treated in isolation.

Our modern healthcare system operates as though none of that were true. It only touches the physical, and when it tries to go deeper and address the mental or emotional, it splinters into separate specialties instead of treating you as the single interconnected system you actually are. It only sees the bright, flashing, loud symptom in front of it, because it doesn't operate from the level of consciousness that would let it ask the harder questions or actually reach the root cause, which is always energetic.

Where it all comes from

The energy animating you was never something you generated on your own.

Yes, you are a physical, energetic, and spiritual being all at once, though it's the spiritual layer that animates your body and acts as a guiding force. If you believe in God, then God — the Creator — is the actual source of that spiritual energy, which means the creative capacity moving through you was never something you generated on your own. It was given to you, which is why your natural state was never meant to be passive. You were built to create, because the energy animating you comes from the Creator, whose entire nature is creation.

To create means to expand, to grow, to learn, to transform, to evolve — that's the actual substance of what creation means, not just making something new but continually becoming something more than you were. Embracing change, then, isn't optional. It's the condition creative energy runs on: it flows through you only as long as you're changing, evolving, and growing, because that motion is what creation actually is.

The reframe

Most of us give our energy to things that never give it back

When we use our energy on things that contract us — that don't expand us or evolve us in a positive direction — like scrolling social media, replaying an old argument, watching news built to scare you, or staying in a relationship or friendship that leaves you drained: we call that normal because it's familiar, but it doesn't mean it's good for us. The truth is that it's actually incredibly harmful to you (and your health) because you're giving your creative energy to things that contract you, and if you continually use your energy to create things that deplete you, you will eventually finance those activities with energy from your cell tissue — which means the cost of creating a life that contracts you no longer stays energetic. It becomes physical.

That physical cost doesn't show up just anywhere in the body, either, because your body is incredibly intelligent and precise — it works like a map and once you learn to read it, each sensation, symptom, or diagnosis tells you something specific about what needs to move — it's a message. Then, once you understand the message and take the action to create, move, and transform in the direction it's pointing — in an expansive way, instead of a contractive one — that energy begins to move, which is often when the symptom starts to clear because the message was integrated and the symptom no longer has anything to say.

Not only that, but we're designed to spend our energy on things that recharge us, like creative pursuits, expansive relationships, fulfilling work, healthy food, clean water, quality sleep, deep spiritual work, movement and exercise, living in the present and not the past. The contradiction, however, is that we want to feel alive, connected, and well, yet we spend all of our energy on things that deplete us. We actually use our creative energy on things that contract us, and when that happens, your body speaks because your soul is sending you a message about how you're living — that you are mismanaging your creative energy.

Voltage, then, isn't just about how much energy you have — it's also about becoming conscious of the direction in which your energy is moving, and how much of it you're holding onto versus how much you're actually using to create, evolve, change, and grow.
